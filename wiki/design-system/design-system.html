01 · The critique

The website was dressed as a bakery. The brand is a fair stand.

The current site uses buttercream backgrounds, a thin editorial serif and soft gold. It looks expensive, but it could be any artisanal Shopify store, and it doesn't look like the stand customers actually walk up to. The real brand has been on the canopy all along: lemon-yellow fabric with polka dots, red script, a black silhouette in a flag hat, heavy slab lettering, under a deep Arizona sky. v3 brings the website back to that.

Keep

  • Queen Red from the logo as the main action color
  • Real photography of Bob, Reina, the kettle and the stand
  • Taste profiles on product cards (now drawn as kernels)
  • Event cards with free-pickup pre-order
  • The warm founder voice: “a happy place”

Retire

  • Buttercream/espresso “artisanal” palette and Instrument Serif. Elegant, but not this brand
  • Soft blurred shadows and glass effects. Our shadows are hard, like a sticker
  • AI-generated photos of people and bags with a fake logo on the label
  • Unverified claims (“#1 Best Seller”, “$34,000 donated”) until Bob & Reina confirm
  • Fade-in-on-scroll sections that look empty on phones

03 · Typography

A slab for the sign, a sans for the menu

The canopy and the logo tagline are set in heavy slab serif, so headlines use Alfa Slab One, a poster slab with fairground signage in its bones. Everything people read or tap uses Figtree, a warm, round sans that stays clear at 15px on a phone in the sun. Two families only. The script lives in the logo image and is never typeset.

04 · Shape & pattern

Stickers, tickets and canopy dots

Three devices taken from the stand give the site its character. Use each where it has a reason to be there, and nowhere else.

Canopy dots

The tent and trailer pattern. Only on yellow fields: hero band, one CTA band, masthead. --dots

Sticker edge

2px ink border + hard 4px drop. For things you press or pick: buttons, product cards, tickets. --border --shadow-sticker

Ticket notch

A fair ticket stub with a perforated date block. Only for events.

07 · Imagery

Real people, real kettle, real sky

Use real photos only. Bright, sunny, close, with hands, paddles, steam and smiles. People buy from Bob and Reina, so show them. Rounded --radius-lg, --shadow-photo.

Do not use

Generated images: hero*.png, flavor-*.png (bags show a fake red-hat logo), story-*.png, process-paddle*.png, variety-mix*.png, product-placeholder.png, royal-seal.png. Replace them with real bag photos (one per flavor, on the yellow tablecloth) when Bob & Reina can shoot them.

08 · Voice

Talk like Bob & Reina at the stand

Friendly, proud, a bit playful, never corporate. “We” is Bob & Reina; “you” is the customer. Short sentences. Facts beat adjectives.

Say

“Hand popped kettle corn, fit for royalty.”

“The Queen's Corn is a happy place.”

“Corn oil, cane sugar, salt. That's it.”

“Pre-order and pick it up warm on Saturday.”

Don't say

“Elevate your snacking journey” · “Premium experiences” · “Artisanal excellence”

Unverified numbers or rankings (“#1”, “$34,000 donated”) until confirmed

Emoji in headings or buttons · ALL-CAPS sentences · exclamation marks in a row!!

09 · Rules for changes

How to change the look without breaking it

Every UI change (people & AI agents)

  1. Use tokens from tokens.css: no raw hex, px font sizes or ad-hoc shadows in components.
  2. Reuse a component from section 06 before inventing one.
  3. Check contrast against the approved pairs table.
  4. Real photos only; confirmed claims only.
  5. Check at 375px and 1440px.

Changing the system itself

  1. Edit wiki/design-system/tokens.css and this page first.
  2. Mirror the change into src/app/globals.css in the same commit.
  3. Record the reason in wiki/decisions/ and a line in wiki/log.md.
  4. Bump the version in the masthead and in tokens.css.
